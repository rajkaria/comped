How it gets the number

No guessing, no sampling, no "anonymous usage". It reads the files your AI tools already wrote and does the sums.

🧾

It reads the receipts

Every request your AI tools make is logged on your computer, with the model used and how much it read and wrote. Nobody looks at those files. comped does — including the extra ones sub-agents leave behind.

🏷️

It prices them at full price

Each request is costed at the provider's published API rates, from a price list bundled with the tool that says where it came from and when. A model it doesn't know is listed, not guessed.

⚖️

It compares to what you pay

Full price ÷ your plan = your comp score. Under it: which AI cost what, what you keep asking your agent to do again, and what changed since you last looked.

Want the exact arithmetic, the rules for collapsing duplicate log lines, and the tests that check it all against an independent tool? That's on the developers page.

What it does with your data: nothing

A tool that reads your logs is asking for trust. So here's the whole deal, in plain words, and a way to check it rather than take our word.

🏠

Stays on your computer

Never sends. No network calls of any kind — not to us, not to anyone. Not "anonymous stats", not a version check. It reads your logs, adds them up, writes one folder and stops.

🔒

Never touches your passwords

Never reads your login files, API keys or any credential, keychain or token file. It doesn't need them: which AI you use comes from the logs, and your plan is never looked up — every plan is priced instead.

✂️

Keeps your prompts to itself

Your messages are cut to 120 characters and hashed before anything is written down. Never the full text, never the code it read, never a file it edited. None of it ever appears on a card.

🔍

You can check

The code is open and has no dependencies. The test suite fails the build if anything in it could make a network call or reach a credential path. That test is 20 lines; you can read it in a minute.

Questions people ask

Is the number a bill? Do I owe this?

No. You're on a subscription; you paid what you paid. The number is what the same work would have cost at the provider's public API prices — it's there so you can see how much your plan is actually worth to you.

Do I need to know how to code?

No. If you use an AI coding tool you've opened Terminal before. Copy one line, paste it, press Enter, say yes when it asks. That's the whole thing.

What is "rote", and why does it get installed?

rote is a free command-line tool from Modiqo that runs small, versioned programs called Plays. comped is one. rote is what shows you what a Play is about to do and asks for your consent before it runs. If you already have it, nothing is installed.

Which tools does it read?

Claude Code (including sub-agent transcripts), Codex CLI, Pi and OpenCode. Ones you don't have are skipped. If you've pointed one of them at another provider — Kimi, GLM, DeepSeek, Gemini and so on — that's detected and priced too.

How does it know my plan?

It doesn't, and it doesn't try to find out — that would mean reading your account, which it refuses to do. Instead it prices every plan your provider sells and marks the most expensive one that fits. Your real score is at least that; find your row for the exact one.

Windows?

Not yet — the runner is Mac and Linux. WSL works.

Can I see it work before pointing it at my own stuff?

Yes. The tool ships with sample logs. The docs have the one-liner that runs on those first.
