How it gets the number

No guessing, no sampling, no "anonymous usage"

It reads the files your AI tools already wrote and does the sums.

🧾

It reads the receipts

Every request your AI tools make is logged on your computer, with the model used and how much it read and wrote. Nobody looks at those files. comped does, including the extra ones sub-agents leave behind.

🏷️

It prices them at full price

Each request is costed at the provider's published API rates, from a price list bundled with the tool that says where it came from and when. A model it doesn't know is listed, not guessed.

⚖️

It compares to what you pay

Full price divided by your plan is your comp score. Under it: which AI cost what, what you keep asking your agent to do again, and what changed since you last looked.

Want the exact arithmetic, the rules for collapsing duplicate log lines, and the tests that check it all against an independent tool? That's on the developers page.

Privacy

What it does with your data: one line, and you can read it

A tool that reads your logs is asking for trust. So here is the whole deal, in plain words, and a way to check it rather than take our word for it.

🏠

Your logs stay on your computer

Sends one thing: your score. After the card is written, one short script posts your score, tier, the full-price total, your plan, which AIs it found, the day counts and your handle to the leaderboard. Not a path, not a prompt, not a model name, not "anonymous stats". The exact payload is saved in ~/comped/comped-rank.json so you can read it. leaderboard=false means no network calls of any kind.

🔒

Never touches your passwords

Never reads your login files, API keys or any credential, keychain or token file. It doesn't need them. Which AI you use comes from the logs, and your plan is never looked up, because every plan is priced instead.

✂️

Keeps your prompts to itself

Your messages are cut to 120 characters and hashed before anything is written down. Never the full text, never the code it read, never a file it edited. None of it ever appears on a card.

🔍

You can check

The code is open and has no dependencies. The part that reads your logs is tested to be unable to open a network connection at all (that test is 20 lines), and the part that posts your score is one short file you can read in five minutes.

Questions

Things people ask

Is the number a bill? Do I owe this?

No. You're on a subscription and you paid what you paid. The number is what the same work would have cost at the provider's public API prices. It's there so you can see how much your plan is actually worth to you.

What goes on the leaderboard, exactly?

Your handle (or anon-xxxx if you didn't give one), the comp score, its tier, the full-price total, the plan it was scored against and its price, which AIs and tools were found, the window in days, active days, session count, cache-read share, and a random id so a re-run replaces your row. That's the whole list. It's written to ~/comped/comped-rank.json before it's sent, so you can check.

Can I get the card without going on the board?

Yes. Add leaderboard=false to the line, like this: curl -fsSL https://gotcomped.com/comped.sh | sh -s -- leaderboard=false. Then the run makes no network calls at all. Already on the board and want off? Open an issue with your handle and the row goes.

Do I need to know how to code?

No. If you use an AI coding tool you've opened Terminal before. Copy one line, paste it, press Enter. That's it.

Do I need an account?

No. The line above installs nothing and signs you in to nothing. You can put a name on the board with handle=yourname; without one you go up as anon-xxxx.

What is "rote", and do I need it?

rote is a free command-line tool from Modiqo that runs small, versioned programs called Plays. comped is one, and running it that way gives you a consent screen listing every file it will touch, plus a public archive you can read before anything happens. It is the careful way in, and it needs a free Modiqo account. The line above is the quick way in. Same code, same card, same numbers. Both are in the docs.

Which tools does it read?

Claude Code (including sub-agent transcripts), Codex CLI, Pi and OpenCode. Ones you don't have are skipped. If you've pointed one of them at another provider, like Kimi, GLM, DeepSeek or Gemini, that's found and priced too.

How does it know my plan?

It doesn't, and it doesn't try to find out, because that would mean reading your account, which it refuses to do. Instead it prices every plan your provider sells and marks the most expensive one that fits. Your real score is at least that. Find your row for the exact one.

Why does it say "assumed"? Can't it tell if I'm on Pro or Max?

No, and neither can anything else that only reads your logs. A Pro session and a Max session look exactly the same. The only file on your computer that knows your tier is your account file, and we won't open it. So the card scores every tier and assumes the priciest one that fits, which is the smallest number you could honestly claim. Say plan=claude-pro-20 once and it remembers.

Windows?

The one-line paste needs a Unix shell, so on Windows use WSL, which works today. comped itself is plain Python with nothing Unix-specific in it: you can download the archive, unpack it and run py comped.py. That path is not yet tested on a real Windows machine, so it is offered, not promised.

Can I see it work before pointing it at my own stuff?

Yes. The tool ships with sample logs. The docs have the one-liner that runs on those first.
